There are six fundamental movements of the hip: flexion, extension, internal rotation, external rotation, adduction, and abduction. I was limited in extension on both sides from sitting all day, and had a lack of IR on the left and ER on the right. I basically never trained adduction and abduction so those muscles were weak also.

### Program design

- Same structure as the upper body: superset a flexibility exercise with strength at end range. Each block has three steps:
	1. **Stretch + PAILs/RAILs:** 2 min passive, then 15 s PAILs (contract the stretched muscle at ~50% effort, pushing against the stretch), then 15 s RAILs (contract the opposing muscle to pull deeper with no help). 2 rounds.
	2. **Loaded eccentric at long length:** tempo 3–4 s down, 1–2 s pause in the stretched position, normal speed up. 6–8 reps, 1–3 reps short of failure.
	3. **End-range lift-offs:** in the deepest position, lift the limb a few cm using only the opposing muscle and hold 3–5 s. If I can't lift at all, hold a max-effort isometric attempt.
- Four sessions a week (~25 min), alternating two days, so each direction gets trained twice a week:
	- **Day A (Mon, Thu):** extension + flexion
	- **Day B (Tue, Fri):** abduction + rotation
- Order within a session: all stretches → loaded lifts → lift-offs.
- Progress range before load. Add depth until the bottom position feels controlled, then add weight.
- Always start with the weaker side, and give it one extra round or set.
- A pinch or hard stop at the front or outside of the hip means joint, not muscle. Change the angle instead of forcing it.
- Running: put reverse Nordics and Cossacks after easy runs, never the day before a workout or long run. Expect soreness in weeks 1–2.
- Retest every 2 weeks: [[L0 - Lower Body Mobility Assessment]]

### Flexion (Day A)
- Target is the hamstrings. They get stretched with the hip flexed and the knee straight.
- Neutral spine. The motion comes from the pelvis tipping forward, not the low back rounding.
- **Supine strap SLR + PAILs/RAILs:** 2 rounds per side
	- Knee locked, other leg flat on the floor.
	- PAILs: press the heel down into the strap. RAILs: lift the leg higher with the hip flexors while the strap only takes up slack.
- **B-stance or single-leg RDL:** 3 × 6–8 per side
	- Hinge until the back starts to round, and stop there.
	- Progress to a deficit (stand on a plate), then add load.
- **Seated straight-leg lift-offs:** 2 × 6 per side
	- Sit tall, legs straight, hands beside the thighs. Lift one heel a few cm without leaning back.
- If bent-knee flexion (knee to chest, bottom of a deep squat) is also limited, the limit is the glutes or posterior hip, not the hamstrings. The 90/90 work under rotation covers that.

### Abduction (Day B)
- Target is the adductors. Train them with the knees both bent (single-joint adductors) and straight (the gracilis, which also crosses the knee).
- Unlike the Day A blocks, tip the pelvis **forward** (stick the butt out), because a middle split needs that tilt.
- **Frog rock-back + PAILs/RAILs:** 2 rounds
	- Knees wide, shins parallel, back flat.
	- PAILs: drive the knees down and in against the floor. RAILs: try to pull the knees wider and sink deeper.
- **Wall straddle (optional):** 3–5 min
	- On my back, butt against the wall, legs up it and falling apart. Covers the straight-knee range, and it's easy to do while reading.
- **Cossack squat:** 3 × 5–6 per side
	- Toes of the straight leg point up.
	- Progress depth (hold a support if needed), then add a goblet weight.
- **Straddle-sit lift-offs:** 2 × 6 per side
	- Sit tall in my widest straddle, hands in front. Lift one heel a few cm and hold.
- Since I never trained adduction or abduction, Copenhagen planks are a good add-on later for adductor strength. They don't work at long length, though.

### Rotation (Day B)
- My limits are IR on the left and ER on the right.
- In a 90/90 sit, the back leg is in IR and the front leg is in ER. Right leg in front with left leg behind stretches both of my limited directions at once, so that orientation gets double time.
- **90/90 sit:** 2 min each orientation, plus an extra 2 min with the right leg in front
	- Sit tall with hips square, using my hands behind me if needed. Don't let the back-leg hip lift off the floor.
- **Seated end-range rotation PAILs/RAILs:** 2 rounds, limited directions only
	- Sit on a bench, thigh horizontal. Rotate to end range: left foot out (IR), right foot in (ER).
	- PAILs: push against my own hand back toward the start. RAILs: let go and actively rotate further.
- **Hip airplanes:** 3 × 5 per side
	- Single-leg hinge, torso level. Rotate the pelvis open, then closed, taking 3–4 s each way with a pause at each end.
	- Hold a support at first. This also trains right-leg stability.
- **Lift-offs:** 2 × 6 per side
	- Left IR: 90/90 back-leg lift-off. Keep the knee down and lift the foot a few cm.
	- Right ER: seated, rotate the foot inward as far as possible and hold 5 s with no help.
