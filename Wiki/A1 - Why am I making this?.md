**My background**

At the 2023 Boston marathon, I ran a lifetime best in the marathon of 2:45:08. I then spend the next 3 years getting slower, and running kept feeling worse. I had recurring hamstring injuries, asymmetric hip and shoulder mobility, and my body just felt off. I struggled to train for triathlon and running consistently.

I had gone into the marathon already compromised. My goal was actually 2:40, and 2:45 was the best I could manage on the day. In the buildup, I had a recurring issue where, after about 10 minutes at goal marathon pace (~6:00/mile), my right hamstring would start feeling like it was cramping and I'd lose control of the whole right leg. I found ways to compensate, but it wasn't pretty. Sure enough, at about mile 4 of that marathon, I was already trying to adjust my gait to make up for a right leg that wasn't functioning properly.

I went to multiple PTs to sort out the weird hamstring issue, but left consistently disappointed. I couldn't point to a specific thing that hurt or reproduce the issues I had when running. I just knew that my body felt weird and had a web of mobility issues that I couldn't make sense of. No PT I worked with did a comprehensive assessment of my body and explained what was going wrong. I'd leave each PT with a few more exercises to do, but my mobility and gait limitations never seemed to improve.

**How to waste your time**

Eventually, I took things into my own hands. I scrolled through exercise and anatomy videos endlessly on Youtube, Instagram, etc, and learned a lot. But I couldn't distill hundreds of exercises into a coherent program that actually helped me make progress. It felt like I was doing everything I could, training multiple times per day but barely making progress.

Even though I felt like I was working hard on fixing my body, I eventually realized that I wasn't *actually* trying. I had never actually measured my mobility, so a lot of the time I was guessing about exercise selection. I also had no idea if my program was working, and never felt confident that I was moving in the right direction. Every few weeks, I'd find a shiny new exercise on Instagram and add it to my program, neglecting to progress an exercise I was already working on or simply forgetting about it.

**The gap between PT and strength training**

If you're like me – not quite injured, but also not moving properly – who do you turn to? The PT and chiropractor industry helps solve pain; billing codes, insurance, and 15-30 minute visits are designed around fixing single, acute issues. They can take you from -1 to 0. Strength coaches and typical lifting routines are good at getting you stronger – they can take you from 1 to 2. But adding strength on top of compensation patterns doesn't help, as I learned the hard way.

There is critical gap between pain-free and actually moving well. What I needed was a program to get me from 0 (pain-free) to 1 (moving properly).

**Building the movement system I wish I had**

What finally got me healthy was:
1. **Measurement**: Frequent assessment of full-body mobility to track improvement and see if my program is working.
2. **Prioritization**: Using assessment to guide exercise selection, and not trying to fix every issue at the same time.
3. **Consistency**: Sticking with the same exercises long enough to see adaptations. Measurement gave me enough trust in my program to actually do this.
