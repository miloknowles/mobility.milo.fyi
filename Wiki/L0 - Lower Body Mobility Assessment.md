## Basics

This assessment covers the lumbar spine and pelvis, hips, knees, ankles, and feet. They limit each other: a squat that fails could be the ankle or the hip, and a tight hip flexor tilts the pelvis and changes how the low back moves. The knee is tested for rotation, which is easy to miss; its flexion is checked through the Thomas test (rectus femoris).

General testing rules are in [[A2 - How to assess mobility]].

Each test is laid out the way the app defines it: ID · movement type · camera view · sides. IDs marked *(in app)* already exist in the app's protocol. Keep them unchanged, since past captures are stored under them.

## Core tests

Run all of these at every retest, in this order. They're grouped by position: standing, then seated on a bench, then lying on the same bench, then on your back on the floor, then face down. Test both sides and start with the weaker one.

### Standing

#### Ankle dorsiflexion (knee to wall)
`ankle-dorsiflexion-wall` · Held position · Side view · Left & right
- **Setup:** Stand barefoot facing a wall in a split stance, with the tested foot in front pointing straight at the wall. Stand side-on to the camera with the tested side closest.
- **Instructions:** Keep your heel down and drive your knee forward toward the wall, in line with your second toe. Slide the foot back until the knee only just touches the wall with the heel still down. Hold.
- **Angle:** Between your shin and vertical. You can also record the toe-to-wall distance.
- **Minimum / ideal:** 35° / 40°+ (roughly 10–12 cm toe-to-wall); sides within 5°
- **Why:** Limited ankle range pushes the work up into the knee and hip in squats, stairs, and running. The wall gives a fixed endpoint, so this is more repeatable than half-kneeling.

#### Toe isolation
`toe-isolation` · Held position · Front view · Left & right
- **Setup:** Stand barefoot facing the camera, with the camera at floor level and close enough to see your toes clearly.
- **Instructions:** (1) Lift your big toe while keeping the other four toes flat on the floor. Hold. (2) Press your big toe down and lift the other four. Hold.
- **Measure:** Pass/fail for each movement. Passing means it's clean, with no help from your hand and no rolling the foot.
- **Minimum / ideal:** Both movements possible / both clean and easy
- **Why:** Independent toe control is a sign of working foot intrinsics, which support the arch and push-off in running.
- **Notes:** This was something I was missing, and fixing it was key.

### Seated (bench or chair)

#### Hip internal rotation (seated, in flexion)
`hip-internal-rotation` *(in app)* · Held position · Front view · Left & right
- **Setup:** Sit on a bench facing the camera with your thigh horizontal and knee at 90°. Keep your thigh still.
- **Instructions:** Rotate your foot outward, which turns the hip inward. Don't lean or hike the hip. Hold.
- **Angle:** Between your shin and vertical.
- **Minimum / ideal:** 35° / 45°; sides within 10°
- **Notes:** My left is limited.

#### Hip external rotation (seated, in flexion)
`hip-external-rotation` *(in app)* · Held position · Front view · Left & right
- **Setup:** Same as internal rotation.
- **Instructions:** Rotate your foot inward, which turns the hip outward. Hold.
- **Angle:** Between your shin and vertical.
- **Minimum / ideal:** 40° / 50°; sides within 10°
- **Notes:** My right is limited.

#### Knee (tibial) internal rotation
`knee-internal-rotation` · Held position · Front view · Left & right
- **Setup:** Sit on a bench or chair with knees at 90° and feet flat. Squeeze a block or both fists between your knees to keep them at a constant width. Set the tripod in front of you, raised and angled down so the foot is clearly visible.
- **Instructions:** Keeping your thigh and knee still, swivel the foot inward as far as you can. Keep the foot flat: don't roll it onto its edge (invert or evert) to get extra range. All the rotation should come from the knee. Hold.
- **Angle:** Between the foot's long axis (heel to second toe) and straight ahead.
- **Minimum / ideal:** 20° / 30°; sides within 10°
- **Why:** The shin rotates under the thigh at every step. Restricted knee rotation pushes that rotation into the foot or the hip.

#### Knee (tibial) external rotation
`knee-external-rotation` · Held position · Front view · Left & right
- **Setup:** Same as knee internal rotation.
- **Instructions:** Swivel the foot outward as far as you can, with the same rules. Hold.
- **Angle:** Same as knee internal rotation.
- **Minimum / ideal:** 30° / 40°; sides within 10°

### Lying on the bench

#### Modified Thomas test
`thomas-test` · Held position · Side view · Left & right
- **Setup:** Lie on your back at the edge of the bench, side-on to the camera. Hug one knee to your chest so your low back stays flat.
- **Instructions:** Let the other leg hang off the bench and relax completely. Hold.
- **Angle:** Between the hanging thigh and horizontal. Also note the knee angle.
- **Minimum / ideal:** Thigh at horizontal / thigh below horizontal, with the knee bent at least ~80°
- **Why:** If the thigh stays up, the hip flexors are tight. If the knee straightens, the rectus femoris is tight. This test is passive, but it has no active equivalent.
- **Notes:** I fail mostly because of the rectus femoris. The app captures one angle per view, so the knee angle may need its own capture.

### Lying on the floor

#### Active straight-leg raise
`active-straight-leg-raise` · Held position · Side view · Left & right
- **Setup:** Lie on your back side-on to the camera, legs straight, pelvis flat.
- **Instructions:** Keep your knee locked and lift one leg as high as you can with control. Keep the other leg flat on the floor. Hold.
- **Angle:** Between the raised leg and the floor.
- **Minimum / ideal:** 70° / 80–90°
- **Why:** Measures usable hamstring and hip flexion range.

#### Wall straddle
`wall-straddle` · Held position · Front view · Both
- **Setup:** Lie on your back with your butt against a wall and legs straight up it. Put the camera at your head end facing the wall.
- **Instructions:** Let your legs fall apart as far as they go, keeping your knees straight. Hold.
- **Angle:** Between your legs.
- **Minimum / ideal:** Beat your baseline (no standard yet)
- **Why:** Adductor length. It's gravity-assisted, but it costs nothing to set up, so it stays in the core battery.

### Lying face down

#### Hip internal rotation (prone, in extension)
`hip-internal-rotation-prone` · Held position · Front view · Left & right
- **Setup:** Lie face down with knees hip-width apart and the tested knee bent to 90°. Put the camera at your feet at shin height.
- **Instructions:** Let the foot fall outward, which turns the hip inward. Keep both hip bones pressed into the floor, without the pelvis lifting or twisting. Hold.
- **Angle:** Between your shin and vertical.
- **Minimum / ideal:** 35° / 45°; sides within 10°
- **Why:** Rotation is limited by different tissues in flexion and in extension. Comparing prone with seated shows where a restriction comes from (see below). Extension is also the position the hip is in when you push off in running.

#### Hip external rotation (prone, in extension)
`hip-external-rotation-prone` · Held position · Front view · Left & right
- **Setup:** Same as prone internal rotation.
- **Instructions:** Let the foot fall inward, across the other leg, which turns the hip outward. Keep the pelvis flat. Hold.
- **Angle:** Between your shin and vertical.
- **Minimum / ideal:** 40° / 50°; sides within 10°

#### Reading hip rotation in flexion vs. extension
- **Limited in both positions, by a similar amount:** probably structural, like the hip joint or the angle of the femur (femoral version). Lots of IR with little ER in both positions suggests the femur is angled forward (anteversion), and the reverse suggests it's angled back (retroversion). That's built-in anatomy to work around, not tightness to stretch.
- **Limited in only one position:** probably soft tissue. If IR is limited only in flexion, look at the back of the hip (posterior capsule, glutes, deep rotators). If it's limited only in extension, look at the front of the hip (anterior capsule, hip flexors).
- **Total arc (IR + ER) similar between the two positions, but split differently:** a sign that the range is shifting with position rather than missing altogether.

## Follow-up tests

Run these only when a core test fails or stops improving (see the active-first rule in [[A2 - How to assess mobility]]).

To work out *why* a test is limited, use a troubleshoot session: [[T1 - Hip Rotation Asymmetry]], [[T2 - Ankle Dorsiflexion]]. See [[T0 - How to troubleshoot]].

| When                          | Test                                    | How                                                                                                                         | Target / interpretation                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Active SLR stalls             | **Passive straight-leg raise** (`passive-straight-leg-raise`) | Same position, with a strap around the foot. Pull the leg up with the strap, knee locked and pelvis flat.         | 80–90°. If passive is well beyond active, you have range you can't control yet. If passive ≈ active, you're at your range limit. |
| Ankle dorsiflexion fails      | **Straight-knee calf stretch**          | Stand facing a wall in a split stance with the back knee straight and heel down. Lean forward and measure the back shin angle. | Compare with the bent-knee result. Limited only with the knee straight points to the gastrocnemius. Limited both ways points to the soleus or the ankle joint. |
| Toe isolation fails, or push-off feels stiff | **Big toe extension**     | Stand barefoot, side-on to the camera. Lift the big toe as high as you can with the ball of the foot down. Measure the toe's angle from the floor. | ≥ 60°. Limited big toe extension blocks a normal push-off in running and walking. |
| Thomas test: knee straightens | **Prone knee bend**                     | Lie face down and bend one knee, bringing your heel toward your butt without your hip lifting.                            | Heel within a hand's width of your butt. Isolates rectus femoris length.                                                   |

**Pain = stop.** If any test reproduces sharp pain (not just stretch), don't push through it. That's a PT question.

## Other tests in the app

These are in the app but aren't in the core battery:
- `ankle-dorsiflexion`: the half-kneeling version, replaced by the knee-to-wall test. Past captures stay under this ID, but they aren't directly comparable with the wall version.

These aren't mobility tests, so they belong to other layers in [[A0 - Training Framework]]:
- `overhead-squat`: a movement-layer pattern test.
- `heel-raise`: calf strength and control, part of the foundational strength layer.
