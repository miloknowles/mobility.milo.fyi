The goal is to make testing fast and repeatable enough that you'll actually retest. A test you only run once tells you almost nothing. These rules apply to every assessment page ([[U0 - Upper Body Mobility Assessment]], [[L0 - Lower Body Mobility Assessment]], etc.).

## Rules

- **Same conditions every time.** Either test cold (first thing, before training) or after the same warmup. Pick one and stick with it. Time of day matters too.
- **Light effort, no forcing.** Move to end range with about 20–30% effort. No bouncing and no cheating with compensations: rib flare, low back arch, shrugging, rotating the torso.
- **Two attempts, record the best.**
- **Always test both sides, starting with the weaker one.** Asymmetry is often more informative than the absolute number. A side-to-side difference of more than ~10° is a priority to address.
- **Pain = stop.** Testing should feel like a stretch at most. Sharp or reproduced pain is a question for a PT.

## Active first, passive only as a follow-up

Core tests are active: you move into the end range under your own power. Active range can never exceed passive range, so passing an active test means you'd pass the passive version too.

The passive version (gravity, a partner, or a weight pulling you into range) doesn't change what you'd train. Whether it's a flexibility or a control problem, the fix is the same: PAILs/RAILs, then load the stretched muscle and the opposing muscle at end range. So passive tests only come in as follow-ups:

- **Progress stalls.** If active range plateaus, passive shows whether you're at your true range limit or still have range you can't control yet.
- **Sanity check.** Very low passive range or a hard, blocked end feel (bone or joint rather than a muscle stretch) probably won't respond to training. See a PT.

## Photos

- Put your phone at the height of the joint being tested, perpendicular to the movement, 2–3 m away, with the whole limb in frame.
- Use the same spot, phone position, and clothing every time. Tight or no shirt is best for shoulder tests.
- Measure angles from the photo with any protractor or angle app. Photos let you compare with old tests, which numbers alone can't do.

## Cadence

- **Baseline:** run the full battery for each body region.
- **Retest:** run the full battery every 2–4 weeks.
- **Spot checks:** re-run one or two priority tests before and after a session to see whether an exercise is actually working for you.
- **Good enough:** once a test meets its target on both sides, switch it to maintenance and move your focus to the next failing test.
