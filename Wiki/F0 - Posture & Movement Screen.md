## Basics

A whole-body screen of held poses, photographed from several directions. It covers two assessment types from [[A0 - Training Framework]]:
- **Posture:** standing alignment, a baseline for all layers.
- **Movement screens:** how you use your range in squat, lunge, single-leg, hinge, and rotation patterns (layer 2).

Unlike the range-of-motion assessments ([[U0 - Upper Body Mobility Assessment]], [[L0 - Lower Body Mobility Assessment]]), most of these shots have no single angle or pass/fail standard. The output is **observations**: asymmetries, shifts, and compensations. Their main use is to point you to which range-of-motion tests and exercises to prioritize, and to compare photos side by side over time.

General testing rules are in [[A2 - How to assess mobility]].

## Setup

- Barefoot, in shorts and a tight shirt or no shirt, so your feet, knees, pelvis, and spine are visible.
- Tripod at hip height, far enough back to fit your whole body with room to squat. **The camera stays put. You turn 90° between views.** Mark your foot position on the floor so it's the same every session.
- Hold each pose for about 2 seconds for the capture. The single-leg squat is the one exception: capture it at the bottom of the movement.

Views: **front** (facing the camera), **back** (facing away), **side** (side-on; left side = your left side toward the camera).

## Poses

18 captures, about 10 minutes.

### Standing posture
`posture-standing` · Held position · Front, back, left side, right side
- **Instructions:** Stand with feet hip-width, arms relaxed at your sides, looking straight ahead. Breathe out and stand naturally rather than "correctly".
- **Front / back, look for:**
  - Head tilt
  - Uneven shoulder height
  - Pelvis not level, or shifted to one side
  - Weight favoring one leg
  - Kneecaps pointing in or out
  - Arches collapsing
  - One foot turned out more than the other
  - Different gaps between arm and torso
- **Sides, look for:**
  - Whether ear, shoulder, hip, knee, and ankle line up vertically
  - Forward head and rounded shoulders
  - Pelvic tilt
  - Knees locked back
  - Swaying the hips forward
  - Differences between the left and right side views, which suggest rotation

### Forward head posture
`forward-head-posture` · Held position · Side view · Both
- **Setup:** Stand side-on to the camera, relaxed, with your head and upper back in frame. Wear a tight shirt or no shirt so the base of your neck is visible.
- **Instructions:** Look straight ahead, let your shoulders relax, and breathe out. Hold.
- **Angle:** Between a horizontal line through the bony bump at the base of your neck (C7) and a line from C7 to your ear hole.
- **Minimum / ideal:** 45° / 50°+
- **Why:** A lower angle means your head sits further in front of your shoulders.
- **Notes:** I started with my ears well in front of my shoulders. One of the few shots here with an angle standard. If it's low, run the neck follow-ups in [[U0 - Upper Body Mobility Assessment]].

### Squat (bottom)
`squat-bottom` · Held position · Front, back, side
- **Instructions:** Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out, arms straight in front. Squat as deep as you can with your heels down and hold at the bottom.
- **Front / back, look for:**
  - Knees caving in or not tracking over the toes
  - Pelvis shifting to one side
  - Weight favoring one foot
  - Arches collapsing
  - One heel lifting
- **Side, look for:**
  - Depth (thigh at or below parallel)
  - Heels lifting
  - Whether the trunk and shin are roughly parallel
  - The low back rounding at the bottom ("butt wink")

### Split squat (bottom)
`split-squat-bottom` · Held position · Front, side · Left & right leg forward
- **Instructions:** Take a long split stance with your feet hip-width apart, not on a tightrope. Lower until the back knee is just above the floor, with your torso upright. Hold.
- **Front, look for:**
  - Front knee caving in or drifting out
  - Pelvis tilted or rotated
  - Trunk leaning sideways
  - Front arch collapsing
- **Side, look for:**
  - Front shin angle and heel staying down
  - Back thigh reaching vertical, which shows hip extension
  - Low back arching to make up for tight hip flexors
  - Trunk leaning forward

### Single-leg squat
`single-leg-squat` · Dynamic movement · Front · Left & right
- **Instructions:** Stand on one leg with the other foot just off the floor in front, arms straight ahead. Squat to about 45–60° of knee bend and come back up. Capture at the bottom.
- **Look for:**
  - Knee caving inward
  - Hip dropping on the free-leg side
  - Trunk leaning over the standing leg
  - Pelvis rotating
  - Arch collapsing
  - Losing balance
  - Differences between legs, which are the main finding
- **Why:** Single-leg loading is what running is made of, and it exposes compensations that a two-leg squat hides.

### Forward fold
`forward-fold` · Held position · Side, back
- **Instructions:** Feet together, knees straight. Bend forward and let your arms hang toward your toes. Hold.
- **Side, look for:**
  - Bending from the hips vs. rounding mostly through the low back
  - Flat or sharply bent sections of the spine
  - Fingertip-to-floor distance
- **Back, look for:**
  - One side of the rib cage or low back sitting higher than the other, a standard screen for spinal asymmetry
  - Pelvis not level

### Standing trunk rotation
`standing-trunk-rotation` · Held position · Front · Left & right
- **Instructions:** Feet hip-width, knees soft. Hold a stick across your chest (or fold your arms). Keeping your feet planted and your head in line with your chest, rotate as far as you can each way and hold.
- **Look for:**
  - Where the rotation comes from. If the pelvis stays square while the shoulders turn, it's coming from the spine. If the pelvis turns a lot, it's coming from the hips. Knees bending or caving and feet rolling are compensations.
  - Using a different strategy to each side
- **Why:** The isolated tests measure T-spine, hip, and knee rotation one at a time. This shows how they combine in a standing turn, which matters for running and swimming. Compare what you see with the isolated results.

## Reading the results

- **Asymmetry is the main signal.** Look for a difference between left and right or front and back, then check the matching range-of-motion tests. For example, a knee caving in on one leg in the single-leg squat → check that side's hip rotation, ankle dorsiflexion, and toe isolation in [[L0 - Lower Body Mobility Assessment]].
- **Compare photos, not numbers.** Put the same pose and view from different sessions side by side.
- **Angles are optional.** Some shots can be annotated where useful, like trunk lean or shin angle, but nothing here requires them.
