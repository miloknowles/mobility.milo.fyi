The spine is broken up into three areas: cervical (neck area), thoracic (shoulder and chest area), and lumbar (lower back area).

## Neck

![[Screenshot 2026-11-02 at 5.53.43 AM.png]]

**Assessment:**
- Forward head posture: Is the chin should be vertically above pecs? And ear roughly above shoulders?
- Rotation: Do you have 80-90 degrees of rotation to the left and right? Is one side worse than the other?
- How much sidebending?

Other notes:
C7 is the most prominent bony point

## Shoulders

**Assessment:**
- Overhead reach: with your back against a wall, can you reach you arms overhead to 180 degrees?
- External rotation: laying on your back, can your hand fall back and touch the ground?

Increasing passive range
- Wall block hold: 2 minutes
	- https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=stXtHIS8fFQ&t=255s
- Hanging cobra: 2 minutes
	- https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=stXtHIS8fFQ&t=255s

Increasing active range
- Wall block PAIL/RAIL: 3 x 10"P/10"R
- Prone swimmers: 5 at high effort
- Stomach overhead press
	- Pause at end range positions
	- Keep legs and glutes engaged
	- Keep core engaged, stomach should be off of the ground and ribs should not be flared. This forces the motion to come from the shoulder extension.
- Weighted thoracic mobility
	- Foam roller underneath upper thoracic spine
	- Knees bent
	- Brace the abs and keep posterior pelvic tilt
